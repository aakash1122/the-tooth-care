## 3. Brand identity

### The core idea

Most clinics in Bashundhara sell equipment and speed.
The Tooth Care sells a relationship.
Dr. Uzzal remembers you, talks to you like a friend, and stays your dentist for years.
Patients relax, open up, and tell him what actually worries them, which is why treatment feels painless and why they come back.

Everything on the website should make a first-time visitor feel that they already know him a little before they walk in.

### Positioning

"Your dentist for life, not a clinic for today."
A personal dental practice led by one named dentist who treats patients like people he will see again, not like walk-in transactions.

### Brand promise

"Cared for, not processed."
Three supporting words: **personal, gentle, honest.**

### Core values (evidence from reviews)

- **He cares.** "Sincere doctor", "cordial and understanding", "made me feel comfortable". This is the headline value.
- **Long relationships.** Reviewers say "I have been receiving treatment here for quite some time" and "no longer afraid of dental visits". People stay.
- **Top-notch behaviour.** Respectful, patient, never rushed. "Rare to find such respectful and expert care."
- **Painless follows from trust.** Anxious patients relax because he talks first and treats second.
- **Honest explanation.** "Explained everything clearly", "gives the best assessment in every situation".

Modern equipment and two branches are supporting facts, not the story.
Mention them, but never lead with them.

### Audience

1. People who have had a bad, rushed, or painful experience at a commercial chain and want a dentist they can trust.
2. Nervous patients and people who avoided the dentist for years.
3. Families who want one dentist for everyone, long term.
4. Expats in Bashundhara and Baridhara who need an English-speaking dentist who treats them as a person, not a foreigner with a wallet.
5. Cosmetic patients who found the clinic through reels and want someone honest about what will and will not work.

### Voice and tone

- First person, from Dr. Uzzal. "I", "you", "we", never "the clinic" or "our facility".
- Warm, unhurried, conversational. Write the way he talks in the chair.
- Honest and plain. Say "this will pinch for a second" rather than "minimally invasive".
- Zero sales pressure. No "limited offer", no "book now" urgency, no countdowns.
- Friendly humour is allowed in small doses (the Facebook posts already do this: "Good luck guessing which teeth aren't real").
- Refer to him as "Dr. Orko Uzzal" once, then "Dr. Uzzal" or simply "I" in his own sections.
- English primary with a Bangla version that reads like spoken Bangla, not a translation.

### Tagline options

- "A dentist who knows your name." (recommended)
- "Come as a patient. Leave as a friend."
- "Dentistry, the way a friend would do it."

Keep the existing "Modern Dentistry, Premium Experience" line only on social media if the clinic wants; it does not fit the website story.

## 4. Design philosophy

### Direction: "A warm room, not a white room"

The site should feel like sitting down for a chat with someone you trust, not like entering a hospital.
Warm, calm, human, a little bit personal, and never sterile.
The test for every screen: does this feel like a person or like an institution?

### Colour

Keep blue for continuity with the logo and signage, but warm everything around it.
Blue on pure white reads as clinical.
The same blue on warm cream with a warm accent reads as friendly and trustworthy.

| Role | Colour | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | Trust navy `#1B3A6B` | Headings, nav, logo. Slightly warmer and softer than the Facebook cover navy |
| Primary light | Friendly blue `#3A6FD8` | Links, icons, secondary buttons |
| Warm accent | Apricot `#F0A868` | Primary buttons (Call, WhatsApp), highlights, the doctor's signature. This is the "human" colour |
| Background | Warm cream `#FBF7F1` | Page background. This single choice does most of the work |
| Surface | Soft white `#FFFDFA` | Cards |
| Text | Warm ink `#1F2733` | Body |
| Muted text | `#6B7280` | Captions |
| Success | Sage `#4E9A6A` | "Open now" pill |

Rules: no pure white backgrounds, no cold greys, no red, no neon.
Warm accent is used sparingly so it stays special.

### Typography

- Headings: a warm, slightly rounded serif such as Fraunces (soft optical size) or Lora. It should feel like a handwritten note, not a textbook.
- Body: a friendly humanist sans such as Nunito or Plus Jakarta Sans. Avoid Inter, which reads corporate.
- One handwriting or signature element only: Dr. Uzzal's real signature scanned as SVG, used under his welcome letter and on the footer. No handwriting fonts elsewhere.
- Bangla: Noto Serif Bengali for headings, Hind Siliguri for body.
- Body 17px, line height 1.7, generous paragraph spacing. Text should feel unhurried.

### Imagery

- The doctor is the hero of the site. Photos of Dr. Uzzal talking with patients, smiling, explaining on a screen, shaking hands, not standing with crossed arms in front of a chair.
- Candid over posed. Slight warm colour grade on all photos so they match the cream background.
- Real patients (with consent) with their names and how long they have been coming. "Rahim, patient since 2021."
- Show the waiting room, tea, the conversation, the follow-up call, not just the equipment.
- Zero gore, no open-mouth surgical close-ups, no stock models.
- Illustrations, if used, should be hand-drawn line style in navy with apricot fills. No flat corporate vector people.

### Layout and motion

- Mobile first, one column, big comfortable tap targets.
- Rounder than usual: 16 to 20px corners on cards and buttons, pill-shaped primary buttons.
- Soft, warm shadows (tinted with the navy at low opacity, not grey).
- Sticky bottom bar on mobile: "Message Dr. Uzzal on WhatsApp" and "Call". The wording matters: message the person, not the clinic.
- Gentle motion only: 200ms fades, a subtle reveal on scroll, nothing bouncy.
- Lots of breathing room. Dense pages feel commercial.

### Micro-copy that carries the brand

- Buttons: "Say hello on WhatsApp", "Call Dr. Uzzal", "Ask a question first". Never "Book now" or "Get started".
- Empty and loading states: "One moment..." instead of spinners with no text.
- Form confirmation: "Thanks, I will reply personally, usually within an hour."
- 404 page: "This page is missing, but I am not. Message me."

### Performance targets (unchanged)

- Static site, no client-side framework on content pages.
- Under 300 KB first load, AVIF/WebP images with explicit sizes.
- Self-hosted subset fonts, `font-display: swap`.
- Lighthouse 95+ on mobile.
- No cookie banner, no chat widget popups, lightweight analytics only.

## 5. Feature set

Every feature is judged by one question: does it make the visitor feel personally cared for?

### Must have (launch)

1. **Home page** ordered as a conversation, not a brochure:
   hero with a candid photo and a one-line welcome in Dr. Uzzal's own words,
   then "What patients say about me" (three reviews, names and how long they have been patients),
   then "How your first visit goes" (three steps: we talk, I explain, we decide together),
   then services, then locations, then a short letter from the doctor.
2. **A letter from Dr. Uzzal** section on the home page and a full page: why he practices this way, what he promises, signed with his real signature.
3. **"Your first visit" page**: exactly what happens, what it costs to just talk, that no treatment starts without explanation. Removes the fear of the unknown.
4. **"Nervous about the dentist?" page**: written directly to anxious patients in his voice, with the phobia reviews quoted.
5. **Patient stories** (not "gallery"): before and after photos paired with a short story and the patient's first name and year they started coming. Consent required.
6. **Direct WhatsApp**: every page has "Message Dr. Uzzal" that opens WhatsApp with a pre-filled friendly greeting. Sticky on mobile.
7. **Services** pages written as answers to questions ("Will it hurt?", "How long?", "What if I wait?"), each ending with "Not sure? Ask me first, no obligation."
8. **Meet Dr. Orko Uzzal**: photo, story, qualifications, BMDC number, and a few personal lines (where he studied, why dentistry, what he does outside the clinic).
9. **Two location pages** with map, landmarks, hours, parking, and a photo of the front door so patients recognise it.
10. **Reviews page** with Google reviews, grouped by theme (caring, painless, long-term patients, international patients).
11. **FAQ** in his voice, with schema markup.
12. **English and Bangla** toggle for home, first visit, services, locations, contact.
13. **Local SEO**: `Dentist` JSON-LD for both branches, per-branch titles.

### Should have (phase 2)

- Simple "Ask Dr. Uzzal a question" form (name, phone, question) delivered to WhatsApp or email. Framed as a conversation, not a booking system.
- Check-up reminder: a form to opt in to a WhatsApp reminder every six months. Directly expresses the long-term relationship.
- "Patient since" badges on stories and reviews.
- Family page: "One dentist for the whole family."
- International patients page written as a personal welcome.
- Short reels embedded in a "From the chair" section.
- Aftercare guides written as notes from the doctor.

### Explicitly out of scope

- Online booking calendars, patient portals, logins, online payment. These feel transactional and are unnecessary for a one-doctor practice that books by phone.
- Chatbots. A bot is the opposite of the brand.
- Pop-ups, promotions, discount banners.

## 7. Open questions for the clinic owner

1. Exact opening time for each branch and weekly off day, if any.
2. Dr. Uzzal's full qualifications and BMDC registration number.
3. A short personal bio: where he studied, why dentistry, anything he is happy to share.
4. A scan or photo of his signature for the letter section.
5. Which number is the WhatsApp number patients should message?
6. Are there other dentists or specialists at either branch?
7. Will the clinic publish prices or price ranges?
8. Consent from patients for before and after photos and first names.
9. A few long-term patients willing to give a short quote and "patient since" year.
10. Preferred domain name.
11. Does the clinic accept bKash or Nagad?
